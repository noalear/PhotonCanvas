# Try laser-induced graphene
<!-- category: Materials and tests -->
<!-- summary: Burn strips at different powers and speeds, and measure which one conducts best. -->
<!-- page: materials-and-tests.md#graphene-test-experimental -->
<!-- order: 72 -->
Experimental: burning cork, wood or polyimide can turn its surface into conductive carbon, for touch
pads and simple sensors. This test finds out whether the blue diode does it on your material. You
need the material on the bed, the machine connected, a multimeter and good ventilation.

## Open the graphene test
<!-- target: ribbon:Laser/Graphene test -->
<!-- wait: window:Graphene test -->
Click **Graphene test** on the **Laser** tab.

## Choose the powers
<!-- target: field:Powers (%) -->
Separated by commas: one column each.

## Choose the speeds
<!-- target: field:Speeds (mm/s) -->
One row each.

## Raise the focus
<!-- target: field:Focus raised (mm) -->
Graphene usually wants a wider, softer spot than engraving.

## Add it
<!-- target: button:Add the test to the design -->
<!-- wait: shapes:+1 -->
The strips go near the top-left of the bed, each numbered.

## Burn it
<!-- target: ribbon:Laser/Ship It -->
Look for a black, velvety burn: grey ash is too much power, brown too little.

## Open the readings
<!-- target: ribbon:Laser/Graphene test -->
<!-- wait: window:Graphene test -->
Click **Graphene test** again: it opens on **Readings**.

## Type the readings
<!-- target: text:Set the multimeter -->
Press a probe on each pad of a strip and type the ohms. Leave it empty if it reads nothing.

## Save the best
<!-- target: button:Save the best to the library -->
The lowest resistance is the best setting.
