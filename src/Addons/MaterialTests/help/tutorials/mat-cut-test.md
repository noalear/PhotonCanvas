# Find the cut
<!-- category: Materials and tests -->
<!-- summary: Cut small squares at different speeds and passes, and keep the quickest one that went through. -->
<!-- page: material-tests.md -->
<!-- order: 30 -->
The best cut is the quickest setting that still goes right through: less char, less time. The cut
test cuts a grid of small squares, and the ones that drop out went through. You need the material on
the honeycomb or pins (air assist on, if you have it) and the machine connected.

## Open the cut test
<!-- target: ribbon:Tests/Cut test -->
<!-- wait: window:Cut test -->
Put the material on the bed, then click **Tests** on the **Materials & Tests** tab, pick **Cut test** in the list and click **Open**.

## Choose the laser
<!-- target: field:Laser -->
Usually the blue diode for wood, acrylic and leather.

## Choose the speeds
<!-- target: field:Across: speed -->
Slowest, fastest and how many: one column each.

## Choose the rows
<!-- target: field:Down: passes or power -->
More passes on each row, or more power. The preview above shows the grid.

## Add it
<!-- target: button:Add the test to the design -->
<!-- wait: shapes:+1 -->
It goes in the middle of the bed, and the window closes.

## Cut it
<!-- target: ribbon:Machine/Ship It -->
<!-- wait: window:Cut test -->
Frame it, **Ship It** and press the machine's button. Stay with it: slow cuts can flare up. When it's
done, the window opens again.

## Mark the ones that went through
<!-- target: tabitem:Read the results -->
Type the measured thickness, then click every square that dropped out or pushes out without tearing.

## Save the cut
<!-- target: button:Save to the library -->
**Use** picks the quickest; choose another if its edges were better. Pick the material and save.
