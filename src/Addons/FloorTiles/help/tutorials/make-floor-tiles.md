# Make a dungeon floor that locks together
<!-- category: Drawing -->
<!-- summary: Make a board of dungeon flagstone floor tiles for a tabletop game, cut into tiles that lock together with jigsaw edges on a one-inch grid. -->
<!-- page: floortiles.md -->
<!-- order: 77 -->
Floor tiles cut from sheet with flagstones etched on make a sturdy, quick floor for a dungeon,
a tavern or a courtyard. Every square of every edge has the same jigsaw joint, so the tiles lock
together whichever way they're turned.

## Open the floor tile maker
<!-- target: ribbon:Designer/Floor tiles -->
<!-- wait: window:Floor tiles -->
Click **Floor tiles** on the **Designer** tab (in the **Terrain** group).

## Set the grid
<!-- target: field:Grid square -->
Type the **Grid square**: 25.4 mm (an inch) suits 28–32 mm figures. Everything lines up on it.

## Size the board
<!-- target: field:Make -->
Leave **Make** on **A board, split into tiles**, type how many **Squares across** and **Squares
down**, and the biggest tile you want (2 × 2 squares is easy to handle and store). The tiles round
the outside get straight edges.

## Choose a texture
<!-- target: tabitem:Texture -->
On the **Texture** tab, pick **Dungeon flagstones**, or flagstones, cobbles, floorboards, brick or
metal. Each tile gets its own layout of stones. **Etch as** *Relief* carves them in depth, each
stone at its own height, instead of etching them flat (much slower).

## Or pick a theme
<!-- target: field:Theme -->
**Theme** at the top of the same tab picks a floor to match the other terrain makers: *Cave* for a
rough rock floor, *Sci-fi* for deck plates, *Desert* for sandstone, *Gothic* for chequered tiles.

## Choose the joints
<!-- target: tabitem:Joints -->
On the **Joints** tab, **Jigsaw edges** push together from above. Choose **Clips** or **Magnets**
if you'd rather pull the floor apart easily.

## Put it in the design
<!-- target: button:Put in the design -->
Press **Put in the design**. The tiles are nested onto the bed, the texture on an etch layer that
runs before the cut. Cut two tiles first and change **Fit** if they're too tight or too loose.
