# Make a broken wall and a rubble pile
<!-- category: Drawing -->
<!-- summary: Make a broken brick wall that matches your walls and slides into their columns, with holes knocked through and a pile of rubble to go with it. -->
<!-- page: ruins.md -->
<!-- order: 80 -->
Ruins go well with whole walls: a broken section in the same bricks, slid into the same columns, and
rubble where it fell.

## Open the ruins maker
<!-- target: ribbon:Home/Ruins -->
<!-- wait: window:Ruins and rubble -->
Click **Ruins** on the **Home** tab.

## Match your walls
<!-- target: button:Match the walls in the design -->
If you've made walls with **Walls**, press **Match the walls in the design**: the ruin takes their
sheet, height, texture and variation, so its bricks line up with theirs.

## Or pick a theme
<!-- target: field:Theme -->
**Theme** at the top of the **Texture** tab gives the ruin a look of its own: dungeon blocks, cave
rock, sci-fi panels (the rubble comes out as twisted plates), sandstone or fine gothic stone.
**Etch as** *Relief* carves the bricks or stones in depth instead of etching them flat (much slower).

## Size the wall and its ends
<!-- target: field:Length -->
Type the wall's **Length** and **Height**. Leave both ends **Whole** to slide the wall into columns,
or make them **Broken** for a wall that stands on its own base.

## Break it
<!-- target: tabitem:Damage -->
On the **Damage** tab, pick how it's broken and how far down, and add **Holes through**,
**Knocked-out stones** and **Cracks**.

## Add a pile of rubble
<!-- target: tabitem:Rubble -->
On the **Rubble** tab, type how many **Piles**, their size and height, and how many **Loose stones**
to scatter round them.

## Put it in the design
<!-- target: button:Put in the design -->
Press **Put in the design**. The pieces are nested onto the bed, the texture on an etch layer that
runs before the cut. Glue each pile's layers in order, on the marks scored on the layer under each one.
